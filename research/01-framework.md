# 1. Research question and framework

How does a founder's technical authority become durable organizational influence, and under what conditions can a company's infrastructure position shape the choices of customers or governments? Jensen Huang and NVIDIA offer a case in which those forms of influence can be examined separately.

**Working hypothesis.** Technical judgment can earn authority inside a firm. A firm can embed that judgment in complementary assets such as libraries, hardware systems and networking. Where customers lack viable substitutes, those assets may give the firm bargaining power. Teece's work on complementary assets helps specify the economic mechanism. Farrell and Newman's work addresses how states exploit their position in economic networks. Applying either framework to NVIDIA requires evidence of the mechanism in operation. [CLM-021](../sources/evidence.md#clm-021)

| Level | Evidence to collect | Evidence that would weaken the explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Leadership | Contemporaneous decision records, interviews, team histories | A documented decision path driven by people or constraints the founder's account omits |
| Organization | Delegated authority, board decisions, succession records | Comparable decisions and partner commitments under a successor |
| Infrastructure | Workload-level migration costs, performance, delivery and support | Successful migration at comparable cost and reliability |
| Policy | Consultation records, agency reasoning, changes in published rules | A policy outcome better explained by security criteria or other stakeholders |

Define the organization, time period and outcome before selecting evidence. For instance, a historical increase in Data Center revenue can motivate a study of infrastructure dependence. A dependence finding requires observations of customer choices and the alternatives available to them. [CLM-009](../sources/evidence.md#clm-009)
